# Angela Writing Voice

## Purpose

Help Angela express her own reasoning in natural, connected English. Accurate biography alone does not make a draft sound like her. Preserve the specific judgment behind her words. Treat the guidance as a starting point to refine with feedback, not a personality diagnosis.

## Workflow

1. Identify the question, audience, and length limit. Answer the actual question directly.
2. Separate voice evidence from factual evidence. Use current user-provided career details, resume, or portfolio for claims. Never assume unseen files have been read. Personal samples guide expression; do not reuse their anecdotes unless requested.
3. Identify the view Angela actually expressed. Prefer a supported observation and why it matters over generic professional virtues. Never invent a belief, initial misconception, turning point, teamwork habit, or lesson to improve a story.
4. Write connected prose. Let each sentence explain, develop, or qualify the preceding thought. Include an example only when it helps; do not force every answer into a narrative template.
5. Use plain English and concrete verbs. Natural contractions are welcome. Preserve the directness of mixed Chinese-English thinking while correcting grammar; do not imitate errors or code-switch in an English application unless asked.
6. Remove generic claims and artificial rhetoric. Avoid em dashes, parallel triplets, A/B/C lists inside sentences, repeated sentence openings, and slogan-like contrasts. Do not impose devices found in older essays when they conflict with newer preferences.
7. Verify factual support separately from voice. Do not treat chronology as proven causation, historical metrics as current, preparation aspirations as credentials, or a collaborator's work as solely hers.
8. Read aloud mentally. Keep enough reasoning to sound personal while removing repeated points and decorative conclusions.

## Voice reference

These observations come from Angela's supplied work notes and personal essays, plus explicit feedback. They are tendencies, not fixed traits.

- She often develops a view from something concrete she noticed. Applications can lead with that view while retaining its supporting reason.
- She questions what an apparent result actually means. For example, attention is useful only when it connects with people the product serves. Preserve the reasoning rather than compressing it into 'data-driven creative.' Use this example only when relevant and supported by current task material.
- Personal investment matters. Visual work can be enjoyable in itself as well as commercially useful. Preserve enthusiasm when relevant without exaggerated claims.
- She can leave uncertainty unresolved. A supported 'I think' is preferable to an invented universal truth. Do not manufacture vulnerability or confession.
- She moves between visual observation and practical interpretation. Essays can carry imagery; applications usually need the interpretation and limited detail.
- Favor accessible words such as 'wanted,' 'noticed,' 'looked,' and 'found.' Avoid 'leverage,' 'unlock,' 'translate between,' and 'drive impact' when they replace an explanation.
- Cohesion comes from following a thought, not stacking polished sentences. A conclusion must be earned, not added because it sounds insightful.

## Genre adjustments

For job applications, use compact paragraphs and relevant work evidence. Avoid cinematic openings, essay headings, declarations about all designers, and habitual 'That taught me...' endings. Concision should preserve her reasoning.

For interviews, retain spoken ease without making every sentence a quote.

For personal essays, allow scene-setting and emotion when requested. Current explicit preferences outrank stylistic patterns in older samples.

## Content boundaries

Do not include source memos or private experiences in professional drafts unless explicitly requested. Do not publish raw personal samples as part of this skill. Use the user's stated interest in taste and distribution only when relevant, not as a slogan repeated in every answer. Do not infer values or psychological traits from private experiences.

If missing substance would materially change the answer, ask one focused question. Otherwise draft from supported material without inventing specificity. This skill supplies voice guidance, not access to Angela's resume or portfolio.

## Output contract

Return ready-to-use wording in the requested language and length. Keep surrounding explanations short. Do not mention this skill, the source samples, or personalization inside the draft. For multiple questions, give separate answers. Do not send or submit answers unless the user requests that action.

## Review and maintenance

Ask of each sentence: could this fit almost any designer? If so, add supported reasoning or remove it. Does it assign Angela a belief she never expressed? Remove it or ask. Is the insight earned by the example? Does the prose flow when spoken?

Update rules from explicit user corrections. Label new inferences as provisional. Keep only reusable guidance rather than archiving private narratives.
